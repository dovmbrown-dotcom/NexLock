Concept Development

Ideate Solution

This phase focused on generating, comparing, and refining possible locking systems for safer cabinet access. Our goal was to move beyond a general idea and identify a solution that could realistically succeed in everyday family homes.

Overview

How the Solution Was Developed

To solve the problem of unsafe cabinet access, we explored multiple design directions rather than settling on the first idea. Each concept was evaluated using clear criteria: safety, compactness, ease of use, simplicity of installation, affordability, and overall practicality for real households. This process helped ensure that the final concept was not only secure, but also realistic for daily use.

Solution Requirements

Key Design Goals

Child Safety

The design must reliably prevent young children from accessing dangerous cabinet contents.

Ease of Use

Adults should be able to unlock and relock the cabinet quickly without frustration.

Compact Design

The mechanism should fit neatly inside a cabinet without taking up significant storage space.

Simple Installation

The product should be practical for home use and not require complicated tools or setup.

Affordability

The design should use realistic components so it can remain cost-effective.

Reliable Daily Function

The system must work consistently enough to become part of a family’s normal routine.

Market Research

Competitors / Existing Solutions

Existing cabinet locks helped define what currently works and what still falls short. Many available child-safety products succeed at blocking access, but often create new problems in convenience, appearance, or usability.

Existing Solution Advantages Limitations
Adhesive strap locks Cheap, simple, widely available Visually bulky, easy to dislike using daily, can wear out over time
Magnetic key locks Hidden design, secure internal locking Require a separate key that can be lost or misplaced
Hook-and-latch cabinet locks Common and effective at limiting access Can be awkward for adults to reach and operate repeatedly
External childproof latches Easy to understand and install Often less attractive and can disrupt the cabinet’s appearance
Concept Generation

Potential Solutions

We developed three possible solutions and compared them based on the design goals above. Each concept approached the problem differently, allowing us to evaluate a range of mechanical, electronic, and security-focused approaches.

Solution 1 — Mechanical Rack & Pinion Lock

Concept 1

Overview: A motorized rack-and-pinion system uses claw hooks to physically block cabinet doors and prevent access to hazardous items.

Strengths

  • Strong mechanical hold
  • Clear physical locking mechanism
  • Durable structure

Limitations

  • Bulky internal system
  • Higher cost
  • Complex installation

Solution 2 — Electromagnet Mount System

Concept 2

Overview: Internal magnets automatically secure the cabinet when closed, with a one-touch external release system for adults.

Strengths

  • Compact and hidden
  • Automatic locking
  • Easy adult access

Limitations

  • Requires power
  • Magnet strength must be tested

Solution 3 — Biometric Deadbolt System

Chosen Design

Overview: A fingerprint-activated deadbolt system allows cabinet access only to authorized users, creating a more secure and technology-driven approach to child safety.

Strengths

  • Personalized access control
  • High level of security
  • Innovative and modern concept
  • Strong product differentiation

Limitations

  • Requires Specific Door Size
  • Requires Power

Innovation

Competitive Advantage

Biometric Access Control

The fingerprint system introduces a level of personalized security that traditional cabinet locks do not provide, ensuring only authorized users can access potentially dangerous items.

Eliminates Universal Unlocking

Unlike standard child locks that can be opened by anyone who understands the mechanism, this design restricts access specifically to approved users.

High-Tech Differentiation

The biometric interface gives the product a modern, innovative identity that clearly stands out from common mechanical or magnetic locking systems.

Stronger Security Positioning

By combining household safety with authentication technology, the design shifts from basic childproofing to a more advanced and controlled safety solution.

Refinement

Design Changes Made Based on Feedback

Feedback played an important role in refining the concept. Early discussion suggested that families would be less likely to use a product that required extra effort every time the cabinet was opened. In response, the design moved toward a one-touch release system with automatic relocking. Feedback also emphasized the importance of keeping the design compact and visually unobtrusive, leading to a stronger focus on hidden internal components rather than bulky external attachments.

  • Reduced emphasis on bulky mechanical components
  • Prioritized one-touch adult access
  • Eliminated removable key dependence
  • Focused more strongly on compact internal placement
  • Added an open-cabinet alert as an extra safety feature

Modern security with a smooth, easy look.
